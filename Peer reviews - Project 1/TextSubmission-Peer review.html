Review:
In general, there are capitalisation and spelling errors in some sections.

  1. Abstract: good overall, but it is not clear where your definition of manipulation comes from. Cite its author or mention it is your own definition.
  2. Introduction: The first paragraph makes a lot of claims about influencing the public opinion and propaganda without any evidence to back it up. It may be a good idea to cite some research there. The research question is kind of "burried" in a wall of text, it may be better to give it a separate paragraph. Additionally, while the RQ is clear, the hypotheses are not very explicit, so it would be a good idea to formulate some predictions about how the different topologies could affect the manipulation.
  3. Methods: "For this paper, we will use a = -1 and b =1, because this is very interpretable and was also used by Bautista (2025)." sounds quite informal at the end, breaking the written structure of this report. Section 3.6 appears to contain an inconsistency regarding the hyperparameters: the paragraph first describes varying the Barabási–Albert parameter k, but then introduces a rewiring probability $p_{r}$ , which appears to belong to the Watts–Strogatz network. Please clarify which parameters were varied for each topology, as this currently makes the experimental setup difficult to reproduce. Moreover, the reproducibility information is not sufficient, as initialization of opinions, the precise assignment of agent types, network generation across seeds, and some details of the parameter sweeps should be specified more explicitly. Also, it should be mentioned more clearly why exactly 10 manipulative agents were used.
  4. The statement that manipulative agents "exert their influence over all the normal agents" seems stronger than what follows from the model itself. Since interactions are restricted by network connectivity and the confidence bound, manipulators cannot directly influence every normal agent. However, if that refers to the indirect influence over time, this would have to be explained more precisely.
  5. The statistical analysis needs more explanation: what are the 100 observations used in each t-test, and are these independent observations? It should also be explained why an independent/paired t-test is appropriate and what assumptions are being made. The statistical testing framework has to be clearly explained and defended.
  6. Topology is confounded with network density and degree distribution, and as these properties themselves affect information propagation, it is difficult to attribute differences in $\Delta \mu$ specifically to topology. While you acknowledges this limitation, it should be addressed more strongly in the experimental design and interpretation of the main results.
  7. Make sure your conclusions fully follow fro the results and literature, as some claims seem stronger than the evidence, e.g. saying that the Barabási result is "supported" by Jalili, although the studies measure different things. Make sure to distinguish between the observed associations and the mechanistic explanations.
  8. Some figures are difficult to interpret without reading the surrounding text. In particular, the axes, hyperparameters, and meaning of the plotted quantities should be made explicit in the figures/captions so that the results are more self-contained. Also each figure needs a caption.
